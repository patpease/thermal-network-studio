# Existing networks near a drawn neighbourhood: the data review

Goal: when a player draws near a thermal network that already exists, show
it — like the heat sources found nearby — with the source cited, and the full
citation on the Learn tab. Two sources were offered; both were checked on
27 September 2026.

## The sources

| | NREL Geothermal Data Repository, submission 1282 | IDEA System Maps |
|---|---|---|
| What | *U.S. Geothermal District Heating and Cooling Systems Data* | Map of North American district energy systems |
| Content | 114 current geothermal heat pump networks (name, state, **latitude, longitude**, a source link); 11 prospective projects (DOE community pilots, town only); 24 direct-use geothermal district heating systems (town, years, temperature, flow, capacity, cost) | District heating, cooling and CHP systems, with capacities |
| As of | 2020, updated 3 March 2026 | 2015 |
| Form | Two Excel files, 64 KB and 249 KB, direct download | A Tableau Public view; no download, no field list |
| Licence | **CC BY 4.0** | None stated; no terms of use or citation guidance on the page |
| Citation | Kolker, A., Beckers, K., & Pauling, H. (2020). *U.S. Geothermal District Heating and Cooling Systems Data*. [Data set]. Geothermal Data Repository. National Renewable Energy Laboratory. https://doi.org/10.15121/1764506 | International District Energy Association, *District Energy Systems Map* (2015), districtenergy.org |

Measured in the NREL files: every one of the 114 current networks has
coordinates; New York holds 20, Illinois 9, Massachusetts 7. One defect found
at a glance — Monroe Community College is labelled MI with coordinates in
Rochester, NY — so labels and coordinates must be checked against each other
on import, not trusted.

## Recommendation

**A dataset in the repo from NREL, generated by a script. No live API call.
IDEA as a link and a citation on Learn only, until IDEA gives permission.**

- **Why not live.** NREL publishes files, not an API, and they change a few
  times a year. A relay call per site would add a failure point for data that
  fits in a 20 KB JSON. It follows the tool's other generated data: a source
  file, a generator that refuses to write what it cannot cite, a committed
  output, a test.
- **Why not copy IDEA.** No licence and no download. Scraping a Tableau
  view's data would copy a compilation IDEA has not licensed. Linking to the
  map and naming it on Learn shares the knowledge without that. If IDEA agrees
  in writing, its systems can join the same dataset with their own citation.
- **Generator** (`npm run import:networks`): download both NREL workbooks
  (the unzip already exists in `scripts/validate/autobem-lib.ts`; an xlsx is
  a zip of XML), read the "Curr TENs" sheet and the direct-use sheet, and
  write `src/site/generated/networks.ts` with name, kind (geothermal heat
  pump network or direct-use district heating), status, state, point, year
  and capacity where given, and the source link. It refuses a row with no
  point, and reports every row whose state label and coordinates disagree —
  recorded as a finding, never corrected quietly.
- **Direct-use systems have a town, not a point.** They would be placed by
  the Census geocoder at generation time, flagged "town centre", and never
  drawn as if the plant's location were known.
- **Prospective projects** (Ann Arbor, Chicago, Duluth …) are named on Learn
  as the DOE pilots, not drawn: a planned network has no site yet.
- **On the map and Site tab**: an "Existing networks nearby" card and markers
  in their own style, apart from connectable heat sources — they are for
  learning, not connecting. "Nearby" wants a wider radius than the quarter
  mile used for sources: 25 miles is proposed, with the nearest few listed and
  their distance in the player's units.
- **Learn**: a fact on how many there are and where, the full NREL citation
  with its DOI, IDEA's map as a further link, and the non-endorsement line
  that already covers every cited organisation.

Effort: about a day — the generator and its test, the card and markers, the
Learn entries, and a browser check.
